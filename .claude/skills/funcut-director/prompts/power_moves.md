# The 100 power moves

One hundred concrete moves across the 25 Director phases. They are a checklist, not a
mandate: use the ones the story earns, and record the ones you deliberately
leave out. "More effects" is not a power move; a move that answers the
narration is.

## Story and brief — phases 0, 1d (1–6)

1. **One-sentence thesis.** Write the sentence the film must leave in the
   viewer's head; every section serves it.
2. **Audience, platform, duration.** Name who watches, where, and for how
   long; the frame, pace and caption size follow from it.
3. **Promise and payoff.** State what the opening promises and where the film
   pays it; cut anything that pays a different promise.
4. **Emotional arc.** Mark the acts and the turn: tension, release, proof,
   lift. The grade, music and cut length follow the arc.
5. **Signature motif.** Choose one repeatable visual or sonic idea the viewer
   can say back; repeat it at the hook, the turn and the close.
6. **Brand and claim guardrails.** List taboos, protected claims and anything
   the film must not imply; the plan's brief carries them.

## Hook and structure — phases 1d, 3 (7–12)

7. **First-frame interrupt.** Open on the strongest image or line, not a
   logo or a slow establishing shot.
8. **Three-second hook.** The first three seconds carry the loudest word, the
   strongest push and the clearest promise.
9. **Open loop.** Ask the question the film answers; do not answer it in the
   first ten seconds.
10. **Chapter turns.** Give each section a visible turn: new place, new scale,
    new colour, new sound, or a hard cut.
11. **Escalation ladder.** Raise one variable per section — stakes, speed,
    scale or intimacy — not all four at once.
12. **Payoff and CTA.** Land the promise, then make one ask; do not end on a
    second, weaker hook.

## Voice and edit — phases 2, 2b, 5 (13–18)

13. **Measured word timing.** Cut and caption from the measured words, never
    interpolated segment times.
14. **Dead air only.** Remove silence and filler, never a dramatic hold.
15. **Protected pauses.** Keep the pause the argument needs; a fast cut is not
    automatically a good cut.
16. **J/L feel.** Let picture lead or voice lead inside the segment; keep the
    plan's segments contiguous.
17. **Cut on action or beat.** Land the cut on a movement, a beat or a
    measured emphasis, not on an arbitrary frame.
18. **Sentence-level pacing.** Match the cut length to the sentence, not to a
    fixed BPM; the voice is the clock.

## Footage — phases 4, 4b, 5 (19–26)

19. **Semantic B-roll.** Every shot answers its sentence; a pretty clip that
    answers nothing is a slot wasted.
20. **Shot-scale ladder.** Alternate wide, medium and close; do not run three
    mediums in a row unless the repetition is the point.
21. **Screen direction.** Keep travel and eyelines consistent; a reversed
    move reads as a mistake.
22. **Colour continuity.** Match the grade direction across adjacent shots;
    a jump in temperature needs a reason.
23. **Motion continuity.** Match camera energy; a locked shot after a whip
    needs a beat of settle.
24. **Hero shot.** Reserve the best frame for the promise or the payoff, not
    the opening logo.
25. **Coverage map.** No narration gap without a planned answer; no shot
    repeated without a new meaning.
26. **Licence triple.** Keep provider, id and url for every chosen clip; a
    monetized brief never shops an unlicensed index.

## Motion — phases 3c, 5b (27–34)

27. **One motion system.** One type behaviour, one easing family, one
    transition logic; variety inside a system, not random presets.
28. **Kinetic type.** Set the stressed words in motion; do not animate the
    whole sentence equally.
29. **Number comps.** Every number, percentage and delta gets a ticker,
    chart or stat comp — never a plain text card.
30. **Comparison comps.** Every before/after, us/them or ranked choice gets a
    split, bar or table that shows the difference.
31. **Process comps.** Every step, flow or timeline gets a diagram that
    reveals in reading order.
32. **Map and location comps.** Every place gets a map, a pin or a route,
    not a generic globe.
33. **Lower thirds and labels.** Name the person, place or source once, at
    the moment it matters, then get out.
34. **Motivated transitions.** A whip, mask or match cut must carry a change
    in place, time or argument; never decorate a straight cut.

## Captions — phase 5c (35–38)

35. **Two-to-four word groups.** One breath or one thought per line; never a
    wall of transcript.
36. **Emphasis styling.** The measured stress word gets the accent; the rest
    stays quiet.
37. **Safe area.** Keep every line inside the platform's safe area and off
    faces, numbers and other captions.
38. **Read-twice hold.** Hold each line long enough to read twice; if it
    cannot, split it.

## Sound — phase 5d (39–43)

39. **BGM gate.** Detect embedded music first; never stack a bed on a take
    that already carries one.
40. **Beat grid.** Cut the bed and the section turns to the measured grid,
    not a guessed BPM.
41. **Ducking by act.** Duck under the voice per act; let the bed lift in the
    gaps that can hold it.
42. **SFX on peaks.** Land each accent on a measured emphasis or cut; no
    machine-gun repetition.
43. **Silence and breath.** Leave air between accents; silence is a sound
    design choice.

## Grade and polish — phases 6, 6b (44–47)

44. **One look.** One grade across the whole runtime; no second filter and no
    five-second layer on a long cut.
45. **Contrast and skin.** Protect skin tones and highlights; a crushed face
    is not a look.
46. **Texture.** Grain, vignette or halation only when it serves the motif;
    never as a default.
47. **A/B proof.** Look at 25% and 75% of the runtime; the same look, the
    right direction, no bars.

## Quality and timeline-ready — phases 7, 7b, 8, 8b, 9 (48–50)

48. **Story gates.** The plan validates, is creativeReady and carries the
    signal log with confidence and basis.
49. **Independent critic and revision.** A fresh-context critic finds the
    highest-leverage problem; you fix it and re-watch the whole cut.
50. **Timeline acceptance.** The saved timeline reads, has clips, has no
    shared lanes and opens for customization; no export is required.

## Scene and beat map — phase 1e (51–55)

51. **Real shot boundaries.** `analyze_scenes` every take; cut on a real
    scene change, not a guessed interval.
52. **Measured beat grid.** `analyze_beats` the bed and the voice; land
    section turns and accents on measured times.
53. **Transition map.** Name the change each transition carries — place,
    time, argument — and choose the transition from that change.
54. **Reframe and mask map.** Mark where a crop, push, mask or tracked
    subject is the only way the shot answers the line.
55. **Energy curve.** Plot the energy through the film; put the biggest
    visual and sonic event at the turn, not at random.

## Geneva deep — phase 3d (56–70)

56. **Read the comp schema.** `motion_inspect` the preset before editing;
    know every slot, node and duration you are about to change.
57. **Preset as a starting point.** `motion_presets` the nearest look, then
    make it this film's comp; never ship the preset untouched.
58. **Custom ops.** Author one validated `ops` batch that changes the
    nodes, slots, timing and easing to the measured numbers.
59. **Slot binding.** Bind the real values — the number, label, unit,
    source — into the comp's slots; no placeholder text survives.
60. **Node graph.** Keep a readable graph: one entry, one reveal, one exit;
    delete the nodes the beat does not need.
61. **Timing and easing.** Set the entrance, hold and exit on the beat;
    choose the curve from the motion skill's library, not a default.
62. **Safe area and legibility.** Keep type inside the platform safe area,
    above the footage's contrast, and readable at phone size.
63. **Frame proof.** `motion_frame` entrance, settled and exit; if the
    middle frame is unreadable, the comp is not done.
64. **Bake at cut size.** `motion_bake` at the cut's exact width, height and
    fps; never scale a different-sized bake into place.
65. **Transparent overlay.** `motion_overlay` when the comp sits over
    footage; check the alpha edge, not just the fill.
66. **Reel storyboard.** `motion_reel` when the section is several scenes;
    one storyboard, one timing spine, no orphan scenes.
67. **External motion.** `motion_import` Lottie/GSAP when the design needs
    it; probe the result and keep the source path in the plan.
68. **Kinetic typography.** Set the stressed word, the reading order and
    the exit as a comp; the caption preset is not the design.
69. **Number and chart comps.** Every number, percentage, comparison,
    ranking, trend or process gets a ticker, chart, triptych or compare.
70. **One motion system.** One easing family, one type behaviour, one
    transition logic across the film; variety inside the system.

## Full editor power — phases 5, 5b, 5c, 5d (71–80)

71. **Masks.** `clip_mask` when a shape, a face or a graphic must share the
    frame; a mask without a reason is a hole.
72. **Subject tracking.** `track_subject` when the mask or label must follow
    a moving person; check the track on the fastest frame.
73. **Templates.** `template_list` / `template_instantiate` when a designed
    opener, lower third or end card saves hours; then customize the copy.
74. **Voice pickups.** `tts_generate` only for a missing pickup or a
    translation; match the take's tone, never replace the real performance.
75. **Transition craft.** Use the served transition catalogue; match the
    transition to the change and keep the cut's rhythm.
76. **Speed and retime.** `clip_speed` for a designed acceleration or hold;
    keep the voice on its measured clock.
77. **Transform and reframe.** `clip_transform` for a deliberate push,
    crop or aspect change; protect faces and safe areas.
78. **Audio repair.** `clip_audio_levels_db`, `effect_layer_add` and
    `measure_loudness` when the take needs repair; never bury a clipped
    word under music.
79. **Scene intelligence.** `analyze_scenes` / `suggest_cuts` when served;
    use the measured boundaries and say when a read was opt-in.
80. **Effects with a job.** Grain, halation, blur and glow only when they
    serve the motif; every layer names the problem it solves.

## Editorial and creative — phases 3, 5 (81–90)

81. **One signature idea.** The cut has one image, sound or line the
    audience can repeat; every section feeds it.
82. **Cold open.** Start on the strongest image or line; earn the context
    after the hook.
83. **Pattern interrupt.** Change scale, sound or speed at the point the
    viewer would predict the next shot.
84. **Escalation.** Raise one variable per section; do not flatten the film
    into the same pace.
85. **Contrast.** Pair wide with close, loud with quiet, fast with hold;
    contrast is the edit.
86. **Visual metaphor.** When the argument is abstract, find a concrete
    image that carries it; do not illustrate it with a text card.
87. **Callback.** Repeat the motif at the turn or the close with a new
    meaning; a repeat without a change is filler.
88. **Silence.** Leave a hole before the payoff; silence is a sound design
    decision, not a gap.
89. **Final line.** End on the sentence the film exists to say; cut the
    second ending.
90. **One ask.** End with one CTA or one question; never two competing
    asks.

## Timeline and acceptance — phases 7, 8, 9 (91–100)

91. **Read the timeline.** `timeline_read` the saved project; confirm the
    clips, captions, comps and audio are the ones you judged.
92. **No shared lanes.** `collision_check`; fix every overlap before
    calling the cut ready.
93. **No accidental gaps.** A gap is a designed hold or a bug; name which.
94. **Persist the plan.** Write `<project>/edit-plan.json`, `plan_validate`
    it and keep it beside the edit.
95. **Receipt, not a promise.** Read `auto-edit-receipt.json`; the
    timeline, story, visual and collision gates are the proof.
96. **Independent critic.** A fresh-context critic gets the contact sheet,
    the brief and the plan; findings, not a rubber stamp.
97. **One revision, then re-watch.** Act on the highest-leverage finding
    and `watch_render` the whole cut again.
98. **No forced export.** The deliverable is the timeline; export only
    when the user asks.
99. **Customization ready.** Leave the timeline clean, named and editable:
    the user can trim, replace, restyle and export.
100. **Honest report.** State the measured score, the gates that passed,
    the ones skipped and the licence provenance; never call a red receipt
    a finished video.
